The idea

Layers, a way in, and what comes out

Data at the bottom, accounts in the middle, the experience on top. A portal cuts through, and above it floats visibility.

04

Construction

One rhythm, one angle

One unit, d, sets every gap, and everything sits on a true 30° isometric grid.

30° isometric

All three layers share the same rounded tile, drawn at the same 30° angle.

d = 24 units

The same gap separates every layer, and the orb from the portal: one steady beat from top to bottom.

ø = portal height

The orb’s diameter matches the portal’s height, so the two read as a pair.

05

Material

Why glass

Portal’s job is visibility, so the layers are see-through: you can look through the experience to what’s underneath.

No outlines

Edges come from colour and transparency alone, keeping the mark soft but precise.

One light source

The only glow comes from the portal’s rim, which picks up all three layer colours.

A clear opening

A flat, deep-purple portal keeps strong contrast on any background.

06

Wordmark & lockups

Typography

“Portal” is set in Agrandir Bold, Auvik’s brand typeface. “Auvik Engineering” is set in Montserrat and spaced to match the wordmark’s width exactly.

07

Usage

Space, size and small screens

Give the mark room, and switch to the solid version when it’s small, in Slack or in print.

Clear space

Keep at least one orb diameter (ø) clear on every side.

Minimum sizes

Use the glass mark at 48px and above. Below that, and for Slack, print and merch, use the solid version.

Solid version

The same shapes in flat colours sampled from the glass mark, so it stays in the family without any transparency.

08

Usage

Please don’t

Keep the mark as drawn. These are the most common ways logos get broken.
